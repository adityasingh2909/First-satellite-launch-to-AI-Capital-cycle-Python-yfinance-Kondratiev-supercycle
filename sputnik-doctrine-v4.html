Where the analogy strains

Divergences, risks,
and the Kondratiev context

The framework is sound. But the one place the Sputnik parallel diverges from the AI capital cycle is also the most financially dangerous. Understanding the gap is the difference between a framework and a trading thesis.

⚠ Key divergence

The software obsolescence risk

Sputnik is a metal sphere in a fixed orbit — physics don't change overnight. But AI is software. DeepSeek R1 (Jan 2025) demonstrated that a ~$6M training run could match GPT-4 performance, briefly raising the question of whether $50,000 H100 clusters are necessary. A geopolitical rival cannot launch a "software Sputnik" that makes rockets obsolete in 72 hours. A software breakthrough can do exactly that to GPU demand. Note: DeepSeek didn't stop the revenue train — Q1 FY27 Revenue was $81.6B (Yahoo Finance).

✓ Confirmed

The "Flopnik" moment has already happened

In Dec 1957, America's Vanguard rocket exploded on live TV — "Flopnik." NVDA's equivalent: DeepSeek shock of Jan 2025. Actual data: Monthly close dropped from $134.29 (Dec 24) → $120.07 (Jan 25) → $108.38 (Mar 25), nadir −19.4% from Dec close. Recovery confirmed: $135.13 (May 25) → $186.58 (Sep 25) → $214.25 (May 26). Flopnik didn't end the space race. It didn't end AI either.

✓ Bullish signal

Institutionalization as a floor, not a ceiling

When NASA was created, US aerospace spending never fell back to pre-Sputnik levels. The CHIPS Act ($52B), Sovereign AI programs, and hyperscaler capex represent structural lock-in. Actual data: NVDA FY2026 FCF $96.7B (Yahoo Finance). Institutional ownership 70.78%. 7,528 institutions hold NVDA shares. The demand floor is set by government mandate and institutional inertia — not speculation.
